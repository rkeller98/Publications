\section{Model, coordinates, and scope}

An \gls{eesm} replaces the fixed PM excitation of a PSM by a controllable rotor
field current.  In the simplest linear steady-state model,
\begin{align}
 \ud&=\Rs\id-\omegae\Lq\iq,\label{eq:eesm-ud}\\
 \uq&=\Rs\iq+\omegae(\Ld\id+\Lexc\iexc),\label{eq:eesm-uq}
\end{align}
where $\Lexc\iexc$ is the field-produced stator flux linkage.  Machine-model
conventions and the limitations of constant inductances are discussed in
standard drive texts \cite{binder2017,schroeder2021}.

\begin{assumption}[Linear stationary EESM]
$L_d,L_q,L_e>0$, $R_s\geq0$, and the flux linkages are linear in the currents.
Rotor-field dynamics are slow or separately controlled, so $i_e$ is a
steady-state decision variable.  Saturation, iron loss, and cross-coupling are
not included.
\end{assumption}

Define
\begin{equation}
 \state=\begin{bmatrix}\id\\\iq\\\iexc\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 \vect u=\begin{bmatrix}\ud\\\uq\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 \Ae=\begin{bmatrix}
 \Rs&-\omegae\Lq&0\\
 \omegae\Ld&\Rs&\omegae\Lexc
 \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \vect u=\Ae\state.\label{eq:eesm-map}
\end{equation}
The essential difference from the PSM affine map is dimensional: $\Ae$ maps
$\R^3$ into $\R^2$.  The extra coordinate is not a small correction to a 2D
ellipse; it changes rank, compactness, and optimization well-posedness.

\physinterpretation A PM flux is essentially fixed, whereas field current is a
controllable flux degree of freedom.  The controller may trade direct-axis
stator current against rotor excitation while preserving the same stator
voltage.

\engineeringconsequence A 3D plot should not be labeled an ellipsoid from its
appearance.  Its quadratic matrix and rank must be examined first.

The development is intentionally pedagogical.  The cylinder will be reached
five ways---dimension and rank, a null direction, eigenvalue signature, SVD,
and stacked fixed-excitation slices---because each view answers a different
engineering question.  The goal is not merely to accept the name of the
surface, but to understand why no ordinary choice of parameters can turn this
voltage-only constraint into a bounded three-dimensional ellipsoid.
