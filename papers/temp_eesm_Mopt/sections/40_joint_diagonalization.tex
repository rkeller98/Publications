\section{Can one constant frame simplify every quadratic form?}
\label{sec:joint-diagonalization}

Loss whitening first maps \(\matr R\) to the identity. Let
\(\widetilde{\matr H}\) and \(\widetilde{\matr Q}_U\) denote the resulting
symmetric torque and voltage matrices. Two real symmetric matrices possess
one common orthogonal eigenbasis exactly when they commute:
\begin{equation}
 [\widetilde{\matr H},\widetilde{\matr Q}_U]
 =\widetilde{\matr H}\widetilde{\matr Q}_U
 -\widetilde{\matr Q}_U\widetilde{\matr H}=\matr0.
 \label{eq:commutator-test}
\end{equation}
The sampled EESM case in §cref{sec:coordinate-validation} gives a nonzero
normalized commutator. Therefore no fixed orthogonal current basis can make
loss, torque, and voltage simultaneously diagonal. This is not a failure of a
clever search; it is the algebraic obstruction. Degenerate eigenvalues permit
rotations within an eigenspace but do not remove the commutator condition
\cite{horn2013}.

\subsection{A fixed best compromise}

An offline \gls{ajd} instead chooses an orthogonal \(\matr S\) to minimize
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\matr S^{\mathsf T}\matr S=\matr I}
 \sum_{k=1}^{N}w_k
 \left\|\operatorname{offdiag}
  (\matr S^{\mathsf T}\matr A_k\matr S)\right\|_F^2.
 \label{eq:ajd-objective}
\end{equation}
The family may contain torque, voltage, and differential-inductance matrices
from representative operating points; the identity loss matrix adds no
preference. Jacobi plane rotations provide a standard signal-processing
algorithm for this problem \cite{cardoso1996joint}. The result is best called
a \emph{machine-normal compromise frame}: it can reduce average cross coupling
but does not assign exact physical meanings, guarantee decoupled dynamics, or
preserve its quality outside the weighted calibration domain.

The residual
\[
 \epsilon_{\mathrm{off}}=
 \frac{\sum_kw_k\|\operatorname{offdiag}
 (\matr S^{\mathsf T}\matr A_k\matr S)\|_F^2}
 {\sum_kw_k\|\matr A_k\|_F^2}
\]
is a useful machine-control co-design descriptor. A smaller value means a
single fixed transformation captures more of the relevant physics.

\subsection{Why not diagonalize online?}

An operating-point eigenframe \(\matr E(\vect i,\omega,M)\) can make one
instantaneous matrix diagonal. It also introduces eigenvector sign ambiguity,
rapid rotations near eigenvalue crossings, interpolation and normalization
cost, and the connection term \(\dot{\matr E}\). A continuous sign convention
and a spectral-gap monitor are mandatory. The moving frame is justified only
if reduced cross coupling overcomes these costs. In practice, three levels are
therefore distinct:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item exact constant frame when commutators vanish;
 \item offline approximate frame when residual coupling is acceptably small;
 \item local moving frame when saturation or active constraints make fixed
       residuals unacceptable.
\end{enumerate}
