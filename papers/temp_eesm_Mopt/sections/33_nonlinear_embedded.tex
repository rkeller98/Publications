\section{Nonlinear geometry and embedded realization}
\label{sec:nonlinear-dynamic-embedded}

For saturation, temperature, and DC-link variation, recompute the local
objects from measured or scheduled parameters:
\begin{align}
 \matr B(\vect i)&=\matr L_{\mathrm{diff}}(\vect i)^{-1},
 &\vect f&=-\matr B\vect r,
 \tag{33.1}\\
 \matr G(\vect i)&=\matr B^{-\mathsf T}\matr S^{-1}\matr B^{-1},
 &\mathcal V(\vect i)&=\vect f+\matr B\mathcal U.
 \tag{33.2}
\end{align}
Here \(R_s(T_s,\omega)\), \(R_f(T_r)\),
\(\matr L_{\mathrm{diff}}(\vect i)\), and
\(U_{\max}(U_{\mathrm{DC}})\) may all vary at runtime.  For three states,
updating \(\vect f\), a Cholesky factor of \(\matr G\), and three principal
directions is a fixed-size operation.  Recomputing a global HJB map or a full
nonlinear geodesic is not.

A safe local nonlinear loop is:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item evaluate flux, differential inductance, resistance, and converter set;
 \item build the local velocity body and task/null-space directions;
 \item take one short policy or optimization step;
 \item verify voltage, current, torque, and original nonlinear flux residuals;
 \item damp or shorten the step if a region boundary is crossed.
\end{enumerate}
This is analogous to the local affine static solver of
Section~\ref{sec:nonlinear}, but now the tangent model predicts
motion rather than a steady operating point.

For an \gls{mcu}, the natural-gradient and moving-manifold laws are the
smallest credible dynamic extensions of the static solver.  For an
\gls{fpga}, symmetric fixed-size factorizations, polynomial flux evaluation,
\gls{cordic} square roots, facet comparisons, and a fixed number of Newton
steps map well to pipelines.  Variable-iteration nonlinear shooting requires
an iteration cap, residual monitor, and feasible fallback.  The time-optimal
deadbeat and MPC literature illustrates the performance--constraint trade-off
for PMSM drives \parencite{lee2014dynamic,brosch2023}.

\subsection{What can be updated cheaply?}

Resistance and DC-link changes update \(\vect f\) and \(\matr S\) directly.
A tabulated reciprocal \(\matr L_{\mathrm{diff}}\) updates the local metric
with one fixed-size factorization.  Fast/slow axes follow from a symmetric
\(3\times3\) eigensolver or from the Cholesky factor without explicit
eigenvectors.  The local natural-gradient direction requires one metric solve.
By contrast, curvature needs spatial derivatives of \(\matr G\), a global
value map needs a grid recomputation, and a changing mechanical speed adds a
state dimension.  The controller should therefore update local geometry
online and reserve global trajectory families for scheduled offline models.

Robustness requires margins on both the velocity body and the path.  If
\(\widehat{\matr L}_{\mathrm{diff}}\) has bounded error, shrink
\(\mathcal U\) or inflate \(\matr G\) so the commanded velocity remains inside
the true body.  Every stored path should carry voltage, current, torque, and
thermal margins, not only a nominal cost.
